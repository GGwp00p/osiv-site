Smart governance of the mango value chain in An Giang

digital traceability, farm logbooks and supply–demand forecasting to reduce market risk

Status
Ongoing · proposal
Partner
Department of Science and Technology, An Giang
illustrative photo

Overview

Traceability in An Giang is often reduced to a QR code on the box. Export buyers and supermarkets, however, ask what is behind it: how the fruit was grown, which crop-protection products were used, whether yields are stable and whether the growing area is large enough.

The second problem is the familiar “good harvest, low price”: many households harvest in the same weeks, nobody knows demand in advance, and farmers depend on traders. The project moves the chain from “grow first, then look for buyers” to “know what the market needs, then organise production”.

Goals

  • Know exactly what the province has: households, hectares, varieties, expected yield and harvest months.
  • Standardise household, orchard and growing-zone codes, varieties, areas and the harvest calendar.
  • Introduce electronic farm logbooks so that each batch of fruit carries its production history.
  • Link cooperatives and buyers through supply–demand forecasting, then batch-level traceability.

Approach

  1. Comparative study of Japan (farm records and product history), Thailand (cooperative coordination) and the Netherlands (forecasting and cold logistics), rated for what can be applied in An Giang now.
  2. Phase one builds data, not software: simple tools (forms, spreadsheets, KoboToolbox, QGIS) to create a verified raw-material-zone database.
  3. Later phases add logbooks, traceability by batch and forecasting; AI and big data come only once the data foundation exists.

Expected outcomes

  • A verified database of the mango growing zone (households, area, orchard and zone codes, varieties, harvest calendar), the chain's first digital asset.
  • Electronic logbook and batch-traceability workflow for cooperatives.
  • Supply–demand forecasting to plan harvests and sales.
