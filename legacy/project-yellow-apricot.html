AI for the indigenous knowledge of yellow-apricot growing in An Nhon Dong

capturing, modelling and transferring artisans' tacit knowledge with a knowledge base and an AI assistant

Overview

The value of an An Nhon Dong yellow-apricot tree depends as much on the artisan as on the plant: choosing varieties, shaping roots and trunks, bending branches, controlling the flowering date, spotting problems and judging artistic and commercial value.

This knowledge is tacit. It passes from master to apprentice by hand and is at risk as older artisans retire. The project turns it into structured, verifiable digital knowledge that can be searched, taught and used to promote the craft.

Goals

  • Capture craft knowledge from at least 5 to 10 leading artisans and households.
  • Convert tacit knowledge into structured data with the SECI model: 20 to 30 technical procedures and at least 50 real knowledge cases.
  • Build a knowledge base with artisan profiles, photos and videos, procedures and 10 to 20 digital tree records linked by QR codes.
  • Develop a domain AI assistant using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) that answers from verified data and cites its sources.
  • Create a digital exhibition space and visual technical library to promote the craft.

Approach

  1. Field surveys and interviews with artisans; every item records who provided it and is classified by knowledge group.
  2. Knowledge modelling with SECI: videos, images, procedures, step diagrams, questions and answers, and technical situations, with standard metadata.
  3. Database and digital tree records with QR codes; RAG-based assistant with source citation, scope control and warnings when data is insufficient.
  4. Pilot with artisans, growers, apprentices and visitors; evaluation, training and transfer.

Expected outcomes

  • A structured, traceable knowledge base of the craft.
  • One domain AI assistant and one knowledge search platform.
  • A digital exhibition space, technical library and working QR system.
