Rainwater coordination for sustainable agriculture in B'Lao

from “drain it fast” to collect, regulate, store, reuse and infiltrate

Status
Ongoing · proposal
Partner
Department of Science and Technology, Lam Dong
Lead
Proposed by the B'Lao Ward People's Committee
illustrative photo

Overview

Greenhouses and sealed surfaces around B'Lao turn rain into fast surface runoff in the wet season, adding to drainage load, erosion and sedimentation, while water runs short in the dry season.

Current measures focus on drainage, saving irrigation water and storage tanks. Collecting, regulating and infiltrating rainwater on site has not yet been tried as a monitored pilot with a clear operating procedure.

Goals

  • Survey drainage, terrain, soils, infiltration capacity and water demand in the pilot area.
  • Build a water-balance model and choose a collect, regulate, store, reuse or infiltrate scheme.
  • Build and operate at least one rainwater-coordination pilot in a representative farming area.
  • Monitor rainfall and the volumes collected, stored and infiltrated, and quantify the effect on runoff.
  • Write procedures for design, operation and maintenance, site-selection criteria and a plan for scaling up.

Approach

  1. Field survey and measurement, rainfall–runoff data and a water-balance model.
  2. Distributed, nature-based measures: roof collection from greenhouses, small regulating ponds, infiltration pits and filter media.
  3. Monitoring system and quantitative evaluation of the pilot.

Expected outcomes

  • One working pilot with monitoring data.
  • A water-balance model and dataset for the area.
  • Technical procedures and site-selection criteria for replication.
